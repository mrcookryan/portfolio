The Shop

Built by a carpenter. Still run like one.

Summit started in a one-bay garage in Longmont. The tools are better now. The standard is the same: measure twice, talk straight, and do not leave a mess for the homeowner to clean up.

  1. 2009

    First invoices under the Summit name. Finish carpentry and repair call-outs.

  2. 2014

    Added a second carpenter. Took on kitchens, baths, and small additions.

  3. 2019

    Moved into the Ironwood Court shop. Exterior crew joined the roster.

  4. 2024

    Still owner-led. Same radius. Same rule: we do not bid work we cannot stand behind.

How a finish carpenter became the contractor neighbors call.

Marcus Hale spent a decade as a finish carpenter before he put his own name on a truck. He was tired of watching good work get undercut by vague scopes and crews that vanished at punch-list time. Summit was the answer: a small shop that would write the plan, build the work, and pick up the phone when something needed a follow-up.

The company is still sized so Marcus can walk every estimate. Estimators who never swing a hammer do not set our prices. If a job is too large or too far, we say so. That habit has kept the calendar honest and the reviews uncoached.

Today the crew covers repair, remodeling, and exterior improvement. Licensed trades — electrical, plumbing, HVAC — are brought in as named partners, not anonymous subs. Homeowners meet the people who will be in the house before the first demo day.

How We Run a Job

Values that show up on site

  • Show up when we say.

    Start windows are written, not implied. If weather or a material delay moves the day, you hear it before the crew is late.

  • Price the job honestly.

    Allowances are labeled. Unknowns behind walls are called out. Change orders are documented before the extra work starts.

  • Protect the house.

    Floors are covered. HVAC returns are filtered. Demo dust is contained. We treat occupied homes as occupied, not as a warehouse.

  • Leave it cleaner.

    A finished job includes a broom-clean site, hardware bagged, and paint touch-ups done before we ask for the final check.

Tradesman philosophy

A good tradesman does not hide behind the punch list. We cut the joint so it fits, we flash the window so it drains, and we stand next to the work when the homeowner walks it. Craft is not a brand word here. It is whether the door still hangs plumb in August.

Marcus Hale, owner & lead carpenter
